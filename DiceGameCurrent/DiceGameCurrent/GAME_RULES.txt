ROLLS — THE COMPLETE RULES
Every game in the suite, in full. Plain text, for copy and paste.
Taken from the shipped code: every number below is the number the app uses.

Games in hub order:
   1. Baseball Dice        7. Freighter.Yacht.Dinghy
   2. Rolls                8. Royal UR
   3. Jackpot              9. Rollbots
   4. Hog                 10. Boneheads
   5. Generals            11. Free Roll
   6. Three Thousand

Every game but Free Roll is two players on one device, passing it back and
forth. A handover screen covers the table between turns, so nobody sees the
other player's choice.


===============================================================================
1. BASEBALL DICE — "Guess the pitch. Take the extra base."
===============================================================================
Players 2 · 2 to 3 dice a throw · 3, 6 or 9 innings

SETUP
  Choose the match length once, before anything else. There is no way back
  to it without starting a new match.
      3 innings   short, about 31 at-bats, roughly 2.8 runs a side
      6 innings   medium, about 59 at-bats, roughly 5.3 runs a side
      9 innings   extra long, about 87 at-bats, roughly 7.9 runs a side
  Three outs to a half-inning. Whoever starts the match pitches first, so
  they bat second — they are the home side, as in real baseball.

THE TURN CYCLE
  1. The pitcher picks a PITCHING STYLE for the whole half-inning, hidden,
     then taps Hide It and hands the device over.
  2. The batter bats until three outs. Each at-bat they pick a SWING without
     seeing the pitch; the app draws the actual pitch from the pitcher's
     style and throws both sides' dice together.
  3. On the third out the player who has just batted picks their own style,
     still holding the device, and hands it back. Roles swap.
  One handover per half-inning. The last batter of the match never picks a
  style, since nobody bats after them.

THE THREE PITCHING STYLES
  A style is a MIX, not a single pitch — the app rolls the actual pitch from
  it every at-bat, so even the pitcher does not know exactly what is coming.
      Style            Fastball  Changeup  Slider
      Power Pitcher      60%       25%      15%
      Crafty             20%       60%      20%
      Breaking Ball      20%       20%      60%

THE CYCLE
  Each pitch beats exactly one swing and loses to exactly one.
      Fastball  beats Power Swing    · loses to Patient Swing
      Changeup  beats Patient Swing  · loses to Contact Swing
      Slider    beats Contact Swing  · loses to Power Swing
  Any other pairing is even.

THE DICE EACH PAIRING THROWS
      Matchup                          Batter            Pitcher
      Favoured (swing beats pitch)     2 x swing die,    1d4
                                       keep the highest
      Even                             1 x swing die     1d8
      Unfavoured — Fastball/Power      1d12              2d12, keep highest
      Unfavoured — Changeup/Patient    1d8               1d10
      Unfavoured — Slider/Contact      1d10              1d12
  The swing dice: Patient d8, Contact d10, Power d12.

THE RESULT — batter's roll minus pitcher's roll
      -5 or worse   Strikeout          1 out
      -4 to +1      Field Out          1 out; see the double play below
      +2            Walk               batter to first, forced runners move 1
      +3 to +4      Single             batter to first, every runner moves 1
      +5            Double             batter to second, every runner moves 2
      +6            Triple             batter to third, every runner scores
      +7 to +9      Deep Out           1 out; a runner on third scores,
                                       unless it was the third out
      +10 or more   Home Run           batter and every runner score

SPECIAL RULES
  Favoured immunity   A favoured swing can never strike out: the worst
                      differential it can produce is 1 - 4 = -3.
  Double max          Both dice of a favoured swing on their maximum is an
                      automatic home run, whatever the table says.
  Double play         A field out with a runner on first is a double play
                      when the PITCHER's roll is even.
  Third-out catch     A deep out that is the third out ends the inning
                      before the runner crosses, so no run scores. The
                      banner says so, because it looks like a bug otherwise.

HOW IT ENDS
  Most runs when the innings run out. A tie plays extra innings, in pairs,
  until someone leads at the end of one.

WORTH KNOWING
  Nothing in this game is a safe pick. Played perfectly, the pitcher's mix
  is 37/30/33 and the batter's 36/26/38 — close to even on both sides, which
  is the point. The Fastball throws two d12 against a Power Swing precisely
  because d12 against d12 was a coin flip rather than a punishment.

2. ROLLS — "One round. Lowest score takes it."
===============================================================================
Players 2 or a whole group · 15 dice · one round each

THE POOL
  Twelve d6, one d8, one d10 and one d12 — fifteen dice.

YOUR TURN
  1. Tap Begin to roll every die in the pool.
  2. Tap any settled die to select it. You must bank at least one die.
  3. Tap Roll to bank the selection and re-throw everything left.
  4. Repeat until every die is banked. Selecting every remaining die turns
     the button into Finish.

SCORING — low is good
  A banked die scores against its OWN best face.
      Rolled the die's highest number     0 points
      One below it                        1 point
      Two below it                        2 points  (and so on)
      Rolled a 1                          the die's full number of sides
  So a 1 costs 6 on a d6, 8 on a d8, 10 on a d10 and 12 on a d12 — the four
  worst results in the game.
      Die       Best face (0 pts)   A 1 costs
      d6 (x12)        6                 6
      d8              8                 8
      d10            10                10
      d12            12                12

HOW IT ENDS
  When all fifteen dice are banked. Lowest total wins.

WORTH KNOWING
  A single round has no turn order, so Rolls is the one game in the suite a
  whole group can play at once — everybody rolls, lowest takes it.

3. JACKPOT — "Five rounds, or hit the jackpot."
===============================================================================
Players 2 · d4, d6, d8, d12, d20 · 5 rounds each, alternating

YOUR TURN
  Roll all five dice together and read SEVEN numbers:
      - the three exposed faces of the d4 (every number except the one
        against the table)
      - the top face of the d6, the d8, the d12 and the d20

SCORING
  Sets      Any number appearing two or more times across your seven scores
            that number times how many times it appears.
                Two 4's           = 8
                Three 3's         = 9
                Four 2's          = 8
            The d4's three faces are always different, so they can only
            match the other dice, never each other.
  Booster   A d20 of 13 or higher adds its own face value to the turn.
  Jackpot   NO sets at all AND a d20 of 12 or lower.

HOW IT ENDS
  A Jackpot wins the game outright — but your opponent always finishes the
  round first. If they Jackpot in the same round, both cancel and play goes
  on. If nobody Jackpots in five rounds, the highest total wins.

WORTH KNOWING
  A Jackpot is about one roll in 23 (4.4%), so roughly a third of games end
  on one. Both players hitting one in the same round happens about twice in
  a thousand games.

4. HOG — "Push your luck to 75."
===============================================================================
Players 2 · one d6 · first to 75

YOUR TURN
  1. Roll the die.
  2. A 2 through 6 adds to your TURN TOTAL. Roll again, or hold.
  3. A 1 — the Hog — ends the turn immediately and the whole turn total is
     gone. Your banked score is untouched.
  4. Holding adds the turn total to your score and passes the die.

HOW IT ENDS
  The first player to bank 75 wins on the spot. There is no answering turn,
  so a lead you can see is a lead you have to catch before they hold.

WORTH KNOWING
  The button says what holding is worth right now ("Hold 14"), and becomes
  "Hold & Win" when banking would end it. A match runs about 15 messages.

5. GENERALS — "One scorecard. Claim it first."
===============================================================================
Players 2 · 5 dice · 12 turns (6 claims each)

THE CARD
  Twelve categories on a SINGLE SHARED scorecard. Each can be claimed once,
  by whoever takes it first. Six claims each, so twelve turns in all.

YOUR TURN
  1. Roll five dice.
  2. Tap the dice you want to keep, then roll the rest. Three rolls in all.
  3. Claim one open category for whatever your hand pays there.

THE CATEGORIES
      Category        Qualifies with                        Scores
      Ones            any dice showing 1                    sum of the 1's
      Twos            any dice showing 2                    sum of the 2's
      Threes          any dice showing 3                    sum of the 3's
      Fours           any dice showing 4                    sum of the 4's
      Fives           any dice showing 5                    sum of the 5's
      Sixes           any dice showing 6                    sum of the 6's
      Set of 3        three of the same face                all five dice
      Set of 4        four of the same face                 all five dice
      Packed House    three of one and a pair of another    25
      Small Joker     four consecutive: 1-2-3-4, 2-3-4-5
                      or 3-4-5-6                            30
      Large Joker     five consecutive: 1-2-3-4-5
                      or 2-3-4-5-6                          40
      Set of 5        all five on the same face             50

ZEROING
  If nothing qualifies you must still claim a category, for zero — and that
  burns it for your opponent too. Which one you give up is a real decision.

TURN ORDER
  A snake: you, them, them, you, you, them, and so on. Six claims each.
  Going first on a shared card is otherwise worth about ten points; the
  snake is what takes that away (measured: 50.0% to the first seat).

HOW IT ENDS
  When all twelve categories are claimed. Highest total wins.

6. THREE THOUSAND — "Bank it or lose it."
===============================================================================
Players 2 · six d6 · first to 3,000

YOUR TURN
  1. Roll the dice you still have.
  2. Set aside at least one scoring die. EVERY die you set aside has to be
     part of a scoring combination — you may keep a single 1, but you may
     not smuggle a 2 out with it.
  3. Roll what is left, or bank.

SCORING
      Each 1                          100
      Each 5                           50
      Three 1's                     1,000
      Three of a kind (2-6)     face x 100   (three 4's = 400)
      Four of a kind                1,000
      Five of a kind                2,000
      Six of a kind                 3,000
      Straight 1-2-3-4-5-6          1,500
      Three pairs                     750

BUSTED
  Roll and find nothing that scores and the turn ends with everything from
  that turn gone. About half of all turns end this way.

HOT DICE
  Set aside all six and you roll all six again with the running total
  intact. There is no limit to how far a turn can run, and no safety in it.

HOW IT ENDS
  Bank 3,000 and your opponent gets ONE last turn to beat it. A tie goes to
  whoever got there first.

WORTH KNOWING
  A banked turn averages about 900. A straight turns up in about one roll of
  six fresh dice in 65, so roughly one game in eight carries one — and at
  1,500 it is half the target.

7. FREIGHTER.YACHT.DINGHY — "Best of three."
===============================================================================
Players 2 · five d6 · 3 rounds each

YOUR TURN
  Four rolls of five dice. You are claiming, strictly in this order:
      Freighter = a 6      then    Yacht = a 5      then    Dinghy = a 4
  After each roll the app sets aside what you need automatically. You cannot
  take a Yacht before you have a Freighter, or a Dinghy before a Yacht — a
  roll full of 5's is worth nothing until a 6 turns up.

CARGO
  Once all three are claimed, the TWO dice left over are your Cargo and
  their total is your score. With rolls left you may rethrow both together —
  both or neither, and whatever comes up stands.

COMING UP SHORT
  Fail to claim all three inside four rolls and the round scores nothing.
  That happens about 31% of the time.

HOW IT ENDS
  Three rounds each, played one at a time so you see every result as it
  lands. Highest total after the third round wins; a tie plays one
  sudden-death round.

WORTH KNOWING
  You qualify about 69% of the time. Cumulative ties over three rounds run
  about 4%, which is why it is best of three rather than a single round.

8. ROYAL UR — "Race, capture, come home."
===============================================================================
Players 2 · five four-sided dice · 3 pieces each

THE RACE
  Three pieces each on a shared board. First to bring TWO of them home wins.
  Your route is sixteen squares: out along your own row, up the middle past
  the bridge, around the far block, and out.

THE DICE
  Roll five d4. Two of each die's four points are marked; count the marked
  points sticking up and that is how far you move.
      Marked up   Move            Chance
          0       0 + Setback      3.1%
          1       1 square        15.6%
          2       2 squares       31.3%
          3       3 squares       31.3%
          4       4 squares       15.6%
          5       5 squares        3.1%

MOVING
  Move ONE piece the full count. A piece waiting to start enters that many
  squares along. You cannot land on your own piece, and you cannot overshoot
  — coming home takes the exact number.
  Tap a piece to select it and its destination lights up; tap the
  destination to commit, or tap another piece to change your mind.

CAPTURE
  Land on your opponent's piece and it goes all the way back to the start.
  Every shared square can be taken, rosettes included — and because the
  final loop runs in opposite directions, the square one step from home is
  exactly where they come in.

ROSETTES
  The five flowered squares fall on the 4th, 8th, 12th and 16th square of
  both routes. Land on one and you roll again.

THE SETBACK
  Roll nothing, or have nowhere legal to go, and you do not simply pass: one
  of your own pieces goes back four squares — or to the start, if something
  is already sitting there. You choose which.

HOW IT ENDS
  The first player with two pieces home wins.

9. ROLLBOTS — "Duel in the net. Answer every shot."
===============================================================================
Players 2 · 16 Integrity Points each · about 12 turns

SETUP
  Each runner picks a WEAPON and one CYBERWARE ability. The second runner
  starts on 17 IP rather than 16, because striking first is worth about four
  percent.

THE WEAPONS
      Weapon         Damage   Special
      Mono-Blade       d8     Cuts through half of any Firewall
      Smart Pistol     d6     Doubled on an accuracy of 7 or more, and that
                              crit cuts armour the same way
      Rail Rifle      d12     No Firewall stops it — but it spends a turn
                              charging first
      Sidearm          d4     No tricks. You never choose it; it is what the
                              Emergency Sub-Routine leaves you holding

ATTACKING
  Throw the accuracy d10 and the weapon's damage die TOGETHER. Both land,
  and the game stops until you tap Send It — so you read your own roll
  before you spend it.

DEFENDING
  The defender sees both numbers before choosing.
      Dodge      d6   Beat their accuracy outright and take nothing. Tie or
                      miss and take all of it.
      Firewall   d8   Subtract the roll from the damage. A natural 8 is a
                      Hard Lockdown and stops everything, the Mono-Blade
                      included.
  The defence die lands and the game stops again until they tap Resolve.

CYBERWARE — one ability, once a match, and every one costs you something
      Ability                 Does                        Costs
      Neural Overclock        Next attack lands at         You cannot defend
                              accuracy 10 and no Firewall  at all on their
                              stops it; the Rail fires     next turn
                              without charging
      Corrosive Virus         3 IP at the start of each    Roll a d6 first:
                              of their next three turns,   on a 1 or 2 it
                              9 in all                     turns on you
      Quantum Phase           Next defence rolls a d12;    Fail the roll and
                              beat their accuracy, take    the attack hits
                              nothing and hit back for 4   you for double
      Emergency Sub-Routine   Repairs 8 IP on the spot     You are down to
                                                           the Sidearm for
                                                           four turns

HOW IT ENDS
  First to take the other to zero. Both at zero in one exchange is a System
  Draw.

WORTH KNOWING
  The three choosable weapons are within a couple of percent of each other
  head to head, and each Cyberware is near break-even — none of them is a
  trap and none is an auto-pick.

10. BONEHEADS — "Stack yours. Smash theirs."
===============================================================================
Players 2 · one d6 · 3 columns of 3 slots each

YOUR TURN
  Roll one die and place it in any of your three columns that still has
  room. That is the whole turn — the only question is which column.

STACKING
  Every number in a column is multiplied by how many times it appears there.
      One 5                 5
      Two 5's              20     (5 x 2, then x2 again)
      Three 5's            45     (5 x 3, then x3 again)
      A 5, a 5 and a 2     22     (20 for the pair, 2 for the single)

SMASHING
  Place a die and EVERY die of that number in your opponent's column of the
  same letter is destroyed. Their survivors close the gap. A three-stack
  they spent three turns building comes apart in one.

HOW IT ENDS
  There are nine slots each, and the moment either player fills their ninth
  the game stops — no answering turn. Highest total wins. That rule is what
  keeps going first from being an advantage (measured: 50.4%).

WORTH KNOWING
  A match is about 23 turns, and a typical winning score is in the forties.
  Each player's dice are their own colour, and a banked die is a copy of the
  one that was thrown.

11. FREE ROLL — "No score, no turns, just dice."
===============================================================================
Players 1 · up to 20 dice · no end

  Pick any mix of d4, d6, d8, d10, d12 and d20 with the + and - controls, up
  to twenty dice in total. Clear empties the table.
  Roll as often as you like. Nothing is banked, nothing is scored, and there
  is no way to lose.
  TOTALS is off by default — the table is the point. Switch it on and every
  throw reports a subtotal per die type ("3d6 · 11") and the throw as a
  whole.
  Dice can be added or removed any time the table is still; wait for a roll
  to settle first.

READING THE DICE
      d4    read at its point — the three faces you can see all show the
            same number beside the top corner, and that is the result
      d6    top face
      d8    top face
      d10   top face
      d12   top face
      d20   top face

ACROSS EVERY GAME
===============================================================================
  Settings (the gear, top right) holds music, sound effects, the table
  colour and How to Play. The table colour and the music are remembered PER
  GAME; the hub has a switch to make the music the same everywhere instead.

  The rules come in two tiers. Inside a game, Settings > Quick Start shows
  the short version for that game alone — the goal, the turn, and the one
  rule that bites. From the game selection screen, Settings > How to Play
  opens a cluster of bubbles, one per game, each opening the full guide:
  every table and every edge case, which is what this document holds.

  Rolls is the only game that takes more than two players.
